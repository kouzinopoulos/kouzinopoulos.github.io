<h3>Objective</h3>

Recent advancements in AI for leukemia management demonstrate that ML and DL techniques, including SVMs, Random Forests and NNs, achieve state-of-the-art predictive performance. However, challenges remain, particularly in domain knowledge (DK) integration, hindering widespread adoption. These challenges include efficient DK representation, effective training in the face of data scarcity, robustness to noise, resilience to adversarial attacks, and lack of model explainability 

<h3>How</h3>

Development of a DK-aware AI framework to enhance accuracy, robustness, and resilience in data-scarce environments. By embedding structured DK into the AI pipeline, improve model generalization, interpretability, and trustworthiness, addressing key challenges in medical AI adoption. DK will be integrated through medical ontologies, including the Orphanet Rare Disease Ontology [1], Human Phenotype Ontology [2], and Gene Ontology [3]. These resources provide standardized and interoperable biomedical knowledge representing complex relationships between diseases, phenotypes, and genes, ensuring semantic consistency and supporting accurate phenotype-genotype correlations for reliable predictive models and clinically relevant insights. The ontologies will be embedded using methods including Onto2Vec [4] and EIKE [5] as well as Box2EL [6] for description logics which encapsulate unique but only partial semantic and structural information within ontologies. 

<h3>Outputs</h3>

Development of a DK-aware AI framework for Leukemia detection. Validation of the framework for different datasets. If the validation yields state of the art results and if time permits, the publication and presentation of the results in an international conference.
